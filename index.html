Features

Everything a field run demands

Six core modules that keep recon, logging, and cataloging connected — even when you are miles from a desk.

🧭

Recon Drill Engine

Run timed field-side observation drills with configurable checkpoints, so every roadside stop sharpens pattern recognition instead of eating the clock.

⚠️

Anomaly Logging

Flag unusual roadside findings with severity, GPS context, and freeform notes. Anomalies stay linked to the exact waypoint where they were spotted.

🔬

Specimen Cataloging

Build a structured catalog of roadside specimens with tags, collection metadata, and photo references — searchable and exportable after the run.

🎞️

Expedition Replay

Rewind any research run along its route, replaying anomalies and catalog entries in sequence to reconstruct what happened and where.

🗺️

Route-Aware Sessions

Group drills, notes, and specimens by expedition leg. Switch between routes without losing context or mixing field data from different runs.

📤

Offline-First Export

Work fully offline on the road, then export clean JSON or CSV datasets for analysis, sharing, or archival once you are back in the lab.

How it works

From install to field-ready in three steps

No accounts, no cloud dependency — the trainer runs locally and keeps your research data on your machine.

1

Download and install

Grab the latest Windows build, run the installer, and launch Roadside Research Trainer. It runs natively on Windows 10 and 11 with no extra dependencies.

2

Set up your expedition

Create a route profile, choose a recon drill preset, and define the anomaly categories you expect to encounter on the open road.

3

Run the route, then replay it

Log anomalies and catalog specimens live during the run, then use Expedition Replay to review the full sequence and export your findings.

FAQ

Questions from the field

Short answers to what researchers ask most before installing.

What exactly is Roadside Research Trainer used for? +

It is a Windows desktop tool for open-road research runs. You use it to run field-side recon drills, log anomalies as you spot them, catalog roadside specimens, and then replay the full expedition to review your findings in order.

Does it need an internet connection in the field? +

No. The trainer is offline-first: all drills, anomaly logs, and specimen records are stored locally on your machine. An internet connection is only useful when you decide to download updates or share exported datasets.
